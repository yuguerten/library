Classics · 1894

Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Vol. III

Karl Marx

Edited by Engels after Marx’s death: the law of the tendency of the rate of profit to fall, the transformation problem, and the inner contradictions of capital.

I · Reading notes

Main points

  • Competition redistributes surplus value through prices of production and average profit.
  • Capitalist development creates tendencies toward a falling profit rate, alongside counteracting forces.
  • Interest, rent and finance divide and obscure the sources of surplus value.
II · Critical perspectives

Criticisms & debates

  • The transformation of values into prices has generated enduring mathematical and conceptual disputes.
  • The falling-profit-rate thesis is contested, and Engels’s assembly of unfinished manuscripts complicates interpretation.

Editorial discussion points, not quotations or a substitute for reading the text.
