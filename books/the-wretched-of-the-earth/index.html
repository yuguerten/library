Modern Left · 1961

The Wretched of the Earth

Frantz Fanon

A landmark of decolonial thought: the psychological and political violence of colonialism, the role of violence in liberation, and the creation of a new humanity.

I · Reading notes

Main points

  • Colonial rule structures both material life and psychological experience through violence.
  • National liberation must confront inequality and the interests of the postcolonial elite.
  • Fanon examines the trauma of colonial conflict and the possibilities of a new humanism.
II · Critical perspectives

Criticisms & debates

  • The account of liberating violence raises ethical questions and can be read as underestimating its destructive aftereffects.
  • The emphasis on national liberation leaves difficult questions about pluralism and post-independence institutions.

Editorial discussion points, not quotations or a substitute for reading the text.
